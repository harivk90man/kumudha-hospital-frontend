Services & Pricing

The catalogue of every billable service plus the rules governing when payment is collected. Lab tests, radiology procedures, and medicines have their own catalogues in Modules 12, 13, and 14 — this module covers the residual: consultations, procedures, ECG, room charges, and similar.

Price architecture decisions (locked):
  • Centralized pricing: every billable item maps to a services row. lab_tests, radiology_procedures, and medicines each carry a service_id FK → services — no separate default_price column on those tables.
  • Doctor consultation fees stay on doctor_profiles: doctor_profiles.consultation_fee and follow_up_fee are the source of truth. Adding a new doctor requires no entry in services. Billing reads the fee directly from doctor_profiles; price history is in audit_logs. No doctor_id column on services.
  • service_billing_policies governs when to collect payment for all service types.
Module 16 contents — one table at a time
  1. services — billable item catalogue (lab tests, radiology, medicines, procedures, room charges)  signed off
  2. service_price_history — closed-period audit of price changes; trigger-driven  signed off
  3. service_billing_policies — dropped; Indian private hospitals always collect before service. Replaced by system_config key billing.payment_before_service = true.

1. services mutable L1 audit L3 audit soft-delete

Master price list for every billable item except doctor consultation fees (those stay on doctor_profiles). Each row covers one service: a lab test, a radiology procedure, a medicine, or a procedure like ECG or dressing. Domain entities point here — lab_tests.service_id, radiology_procedures.service_id, medicines.service_id — so billing always resolves price from one place.

Every invoice_items row (Module 17) links to a services row and snapshots the price at billing time. Price changes trigger an insert into service_price_history automatically.

2. service_price_history closed-period L1 audit L3 audit

Closed-period audit of every price change on services. Populated entirely by the trigger tr_services_au_price_history — no app code writes here. At any point in time, the row where effective_to IS NULL is the currently active price; older rows are sealed with their end date.

Used for invoice repricing audits ("what was the price of ECG on 15 March?") and the owner's price trend reports.

Append-with-one-exception pattern: new rows are inserted by trigger (append). The only allowed UPDATE is setting effective_to on the previously-open row when a new price takes effect. All other UPDATEs and all DELETEs are blocked by trigger.

3. service_billing_policies mutable L1 audit L3 audit soft-delete

Rules engine — answers "when do we collect payment for this service?" One row per service_type (tenant-wide default) with optional per-department overrides. At the moment a service is created (lab order, radiology order, pharmacy sale), the app reads this table to set the payment timing and calculate any advance requirement.

Lookup priority: department-specific row first → fall back to tenant-wide default (department_id IS NULL) → if neither found, treat as after_service.

Note: service_type here is a superset of services.service_type — it includes op_consultation and op_followup so billing policy applies to consultation fees (priced from doctor_profiles) as well as all services-table items.
